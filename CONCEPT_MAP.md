# Concept map: questions rather than architecture

This map is intentionally nonhierarchical. It collects hypotheses that may be mutually incompatible.

| Idea | Question | Failure mode / objection |
|---|---|---|
| Practical possibility space | What can system S achieve under resources R? | Depends on how goals, success, and budgets are defined |
| Transient trajectory | Can a perturbation reveal a useful *present* continuation? | Observability and action latency may make it useless |
| Recursive molting | Can a system leave a previous state-space description behind? | May be only reparameterization in a larger fixed space |
| Representational collapse | Can a new representation make families of problems trivial? | Complexity may be moved into encoding/decoding |
| Boundary migration | Can a formerly binding learnability limit cease to bind? | A new method may still pay an equivalent cost elsewhere |
| Substrate reorganization | Can memory, computation, and adaptation exchange roles? | Physical coupling and resource constraints remain |
| Noncentral cognition | Can problem solving lack a privileged controller? | Coordination overhead and accountability remain |
| Open-ended operation discovery | Can systems invent useful transformation primitives? | Search and validation may cost more than benefit |
| Cross-molt continuity | What survives when the prior coordinate system does not? | Continuity may be undefined or experimentally unverifiable |
| Evaluation across moltings | What counts as improvement if measures change? | Self-serving evaluator drift and Goodhart effects |

## Relationships worth testing

- Molting **might** alter which transient trajectories are accessible.
- Novel transformation primitives **might** trigger molting—or might simply improve navigation within a fixed space.
- A boundary can be **learner-relative** without being physically illusory.
- Some apparently expanded spaces may be *compressed descriptions* of unchanged possibilities.
- A record of previous moltings may be a useful map, misleading residue, or merely historical evidence.
- Continuous adaptation does not require consciousness, biological personhood, or an enduring self.

## The hard question

What makes a transition a **change of state space**, rather than a change of state, a coordinate transformation, a change of model class, or an observer losing track?

Any formalization should make this distinction explicit.
