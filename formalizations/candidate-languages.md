# Candidate formal languages—and where they betray the metaphor

**Status:** sketches for critique, not derived theorems.

## Model A: regimes and transformations

Represent regime i by D_i = (S_i, T_i, E_i), where S_i is a descriptive state domain, T_i accessible transformations, and E_i comparison/evaluation criteria.

A molt is provisionally written:

    M_i: D_i -> D_(i+1)

Potential strength: it exposes which assumptions change.

Potential failure: writing M_i as an arrow presupposes a common meta-description containing both endpoints and a well-formed transformation. The central intuition may reject exactly this.

## Model B: observer-relative state spaces

Let O_i be an observer's representational apparatus and X_t an underlying process. The observer constructs an operative state space S(O_i, X_t).

Then molting could mean that O_i ceases to be adequate, requiring O_(i+1). In this account **the process need not leave a literal state space**; the observer changes its descriptive framework.

Potential strength: scientifically conservative and operationalizable.

Potential failure: it may domesticate genuine transformation into mere epistemic revision.

## Model C: transient action relations

Instead of demanding a globally fixed transition map, define a local relation A_t linking current observations, interventions, and available actions:

    A_t(o, p) = candidate actionable continuations

At t+Δ the relation may have changed. Practical utility is evaluated by downstream outcomes, including observation/probing cost.

Potential strength: focuses on the present trajectory and avoids needing a universal map in the learner's workflow.

Potential failure: an external theorist can still embed every A_t within an ordinary history-dependent transition system.

## Model D: a family of partially translatable descriptions

For different regimes, seek partial maps F_(i,j) relating only some operations, observations, or outcomes. Do not assume all pairs are mutually translatable.

Potential strength: provides a research vocabulary for continuity without complete coordinate equivalence.

Potential failure: 'partial translation' can become unfalsifiable unless the preserved observable relationships are specified.

## Model E: process-first formulation

Take events and interactions as primary, with 'states' reconstructed as convenient summaries. The ring is a record of a previous summarization; the ball is the unresolved generative process.

Potential strength: may preserve the intuition that state descriptions are consequences of interacting with the process.

Potential failure: process-first vocabulary by itself yields no prediction and can merely rename a dynamical system.

## What would make one of these useful?

A candidate must outperform a simpler model on a precisely stated criterion: explanation, compression, predictions on held-out transitions, resource-bounded intervention, or identification of blind spots. Failure to do so does not prove the metaphor worthless; it means that particular formalization hasn't earned its complexity.

## Anti-overclaim

No mathematical framework above proves that ontologically new state spaces are generated, that new computable functions emerge, or that future AI will exhibit molting.
