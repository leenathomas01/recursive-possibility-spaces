# Recursive molting: a ball and its rings

**Status:** conceptual metaphor; not a physical or computational discovery.

## Origin image

A ball pulses. A ring propagates outward as the ball moves further inward. Attention returns to the ball; it pulses again, moves further in, and leaves another ring. Each ring resembles a shed state-space description. The process may recur.

The image is deliberately not a depiction of ever-larger circles of capability. **Outward ring propagation is not proof of growth; inward motion is not a literal spatial claim.**

## Interpretation

- The **ball** is the currently active generative process, not a fixed identity or a homunculus.
- The **pulse** is a perturbation event that reveals a presently accessible trajectory.
- A **ring** is an earlier descriptive envelope, regime, or state-space formalization.
- A **molt** is a transition after which the old envelope ceases to adequately characterize relevant operations.
- **Recursion** means that transformation of the descriptive/operational domain may itself be repeated or transformed.

The rings might be historical records, active interface constraints, or something else. The metaphor does not settle this.

## A provisional formal scaffold

Let S_i be a domain used to describe the system's operative possibilities during regime i. Let M_i denote a transformation leading to regime i+1:

    (S_i, T_i, E_i) --M_i--> (S_(i+1), T_(i+1), E_(i+1))

T denotes accessible transformations; E denotes evaluation criteria.

**Warning:** this notation quietly assumes comparable, indexable regimes. It is a scaffold to criticize, not the theory. M_i need not be invertible, injective, surjective, continuous, or even meaningful in the same language as M_(i+1).

## What survives?

Possible continuities include information, function, behavior, causal dependency, or recoverable translation. None by itself guarantees personal identity or measurable improvement.

One research challenge is to identify minimal *cross-regime witnesses*: observable relationships that allow a before/after statement without demanding a universal shared coordinate system.

## The strongest rival explanation

A sufficiently large fixed meta-space could contain every apparent molt as an ordinary state change. In that case 'molting' is epistemic: only the observer's working description is being shed.

That does not make the concept useless; it changes the claim. A rigorous account must tell **ontological molting** apart from **model-relative molting**—or explicitly leave the distinction unresolved.

## Failure of the RSI interpretation

A system might molt endlessly while becoming less reliable, less capable, or incomparable by any meaningful standard. Molting is a generative hypothesis, not an optimization theorem.

## Questions

1. Must a ring remain interpretable after a molt?
2. Are rings inert traces or active constraints?
3. Can old regimes be revisited?
4. What would count as the *same* transformation when the transformation vocabulary changes?
5. Can an observer tell a genuine regime transition from an adversarial change of representation?
