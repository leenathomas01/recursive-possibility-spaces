# Operations, substrates, and the movable boundaries of computation

**Status:** speculative extrapolation with partial precedents.

## Provocation

Human-designed AI workflows usually divide training, inference, memory, model architecture, optimization, and evaluation. Which divisions are necessary, and which are implementation conventions?

An AI might discover ways of transforming an internal representation that do not correspond neatly to human-authored programs, natural-language steps, or gradient updates.

## Candidate possibilities

**Problem-space collapse.** A representation maps many superficially different tasks onto a smaller set of transformations. The challenge is to include the cost of learning, compiling, and validating the map.

**Operation discovery.** A learner discovers primitives for manipulating representations; those primitives alter the future search for other primitives. The challenge is distinguishing real capability gains from benchmark exploitation.

**Cognition without a privileged center.** Distributed processes coordinate without a single permanent controller. This is not the same as claiming there is no orchestration or no causal bottleneck.

**Substrate reorganization.** Storage, computation, and adaptive rules are allowed to exchange functions. Such coupling has partial analogues in physical computing, neuromorphic hardware, recurrent networks, and adaptive materials, but broad self-reorganizing 'organisms' remain speculative.

**Recursive operation-space change.** A system changes the ways it can modify itself. Repeated transformation need not preserve the language in which earlier modifications were described.

## Why weight-space programming is relevant but not decisive

Research on direct model-weight interventions suggests an alternative to treating training by gradient descent as the only way to change learned behavior. That does not show arbitrary, reliable editing of capabilities, let alone open-ended RSI.

The larger question is whether learned interventions could alter not just parameters but **future discoverability of useful interventions**.

## Important boundaries

- A substrate is subject to thermodynamics, noise, energy and latency costs.
- A mathematical analogy between dynamics and intelligence is not evidence of implemented cognition.
- Self-rewriting evaluation criteria risk rewarding arbitrary drift.
- 'Machine-native' does not automatically mean superior, safe, or comprehensible.

## Open question

If all our categories are tools for describing a computational process, what evidence would justify replacing them with a newly discovered set rather than merely renaming familiar operations?
