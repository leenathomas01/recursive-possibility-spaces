## Premise

If neural model modifications can be found, described, and reapplied, they might be stored like books: retrieved, examined, adapted to other architectures, composed, rejected, or preserved simply for their historical value. This is a design hypothesis, not a demonstrated universal capability.

## Claims worth separating

1. Repair of a dysfunctional circuit may expose an ability already available but blocked in expression; building a previously absent mechanism is different.
2. A method describing how to construct an intervention might transfer across architectures better than a literal weight delta, but that portability has not been established generally.
3. Storage is not deployment. Admission to an archive need not be conditioned on immediate utility; application must undergo compatibility, validation, provenance, and authorization checks.
4. A historical entry can be meaningful without being installable.
